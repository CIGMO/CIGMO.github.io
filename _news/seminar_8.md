#### _Mesh-dependent iteration count growth in primal-dual active set strategies_

- **Speaker**: [Ioannis P. A. Papadopoulos](https://ioannispapapadopoulos.github.io/) (University of Oxford, Oxford, UK)
- **Date**: October 5, 2026
- **Time**: 11:00 -- 12:00 (EST)
- **Abstract**:
  Quadratic programming problems often arise in mathematical models, and primal-dual active set (PDAS) strategies provide a highly effective algorithm for their solution. For finite-dimensional problems, an equivalence between PDAS and semismooth Newton methods was established in 2002, explaining the fast local superlinear convergence observed for these methods. This connection has also been shown to hold for certain infinite-dimensional problems, such as optimal control problems with control constraints.
  On the other hand, PDAS is not a semismooth Newton method when applied to the infinite-dimensional obstacle problem. It is nevertheless a semismooth Newton method for any finite-dimensional discretization, and local superlinear convergence is therefore observed in practice. But there is a catch: with each uniform refinement of the mesh, the number of iterations required for convergence grows dramatically, eventually doubling with each refinement.
  This phenomenon has been occasionally noted in the literature but never fully investigated. In this talk, we provide a detailed explanation of why this mesh-dependent behaviour occurs. We also investigate PDAS applied to the Signorini problem, where the convergence behaviour is also mesh-dependent but fundamentally different from that of the obstacle problem.
  Preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26622.
